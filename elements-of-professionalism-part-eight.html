The Elements of Professionalism · Part Eight

Your Word and Your Work

Friday, October 16, 2026

Team,

I want to talk this week about something simple. As people, there are really only two things we have to offer the world: our word and our work. Everything else can be taken away or changed. Those two belong to us.

That means everything you put out into the world has your name on it, whether you typed your name at the bottom or not. Every email, every text, every work order note, every inspection photo, every phone call. Each one is you signing your name.

All we have is our word and our work. Whatever you put out, you are signing your own name to it.

Your word

Your word is every promise you make, including the small ones. "I'll call you back this afternoon." "I'll have that quote to you by Thursday." "I'll check on it and let you know."

The person on the other end hears a commitment from you. When you keep it, they learn they can rely on you. When you miss it, they learn the opposite, even if nobody says anything. Most people will never tell you they stopped trusting what you say. They just stop believing it.

So before you promise something, make sure you can deliver it. If you can't, say what you can do instead. An honest "I won't have it today, but you'll have it by noon tomorrow" is worth far more than a promise you are hoping to keep.

Your work

Your work is everything you produce. Here is where I want each of you to slow down and ask one question before anything leaves your hands: would I be proud to have my name printed at the top of this?

  • An email to an owner with typos or missing details tells them we were rushing, and they wonder what else we rushed.
  • A work order note that says "fixed" tells the next person nothing. A note that says what was wrong, what was done and what it cost tells the whole story.
  • Move-in and move-out photos may be the evidence in a deposit dispute months from now. We don't take them, but we do review them, and that review has your name on it. Someone you have never met may judge our company by how carefully you checked them.
  • A listing is the first thing a renter ever sees from us. Its accuracy and care set their expectations for everything after.

None of this needs to be perfect. It needs to be accurate, complete and done with care. That is the standard.

The world notices

Owners notice. Residents notice. Vendors, HOA managers and other agents notice. They notice when you call back when you said you would, and they notice when you don't. They notice a clear, careful email, and they notice a sloppy one.

And they talk. Much of our new business comes from referrals: an agent who trusts us, an owner who tells a neighbor, a resident who becomes an owner years later. Verandah's reputation was built over more than two decades, one conversation and one piece of work at a time. Every one of you adds to it every day.

The good news is that the noticing works in your favor too. Careful, reliable people get noticed. I see it, our owners see it, and it comes back to all of us.

Be proud of who you are

I want you to be proud of yourself and of what you represent. Verandah stands for being selective, being honest with owners even when the news is hard, and taking care of properties as if they were our own. When you do your work well, you are not only representing the company. You are showing people who you are.

That pride is not about being perfect or never making a mistake. It is about caring enough to get it right, and owning it when you don't.

This week, before you send or submit anything
  1. Is it accurate? Names, addresses, numbers and dates are right.
  2. Is it complete? The person reading it won't have to come back with a question you could have answered.
  3. Would I sign my name to it? If the answer is no, take two more minutes.

Thank you for the work you do. I notice it, and I am proud to have my name next to yours.
